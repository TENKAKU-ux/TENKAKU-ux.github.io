Furusato Nozei Map Hometown tax balances on a map

Some local governments collect large donations through the hometown tax program, while others lose resident tax revenue when their residents donate elsewhere. Based on the Ministry's survey of the program, this site puts each local government's donations received next to its resident tax deductions and shows the difference as a color-coded map of Japan and nationwide rankings. You can also see per-capita figures, a breakdown of solicitation costs, and how donations have changed since fiscal 2008, and every page has a shareable URL. So that AI agents can look up the same data, it also offers WebMCP tools in the browser and an MCP server you can register in tools such as Claude Code. The site is unofficial, and its sources are the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, e-Stat, and the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan. The site itself is in Japanese.

Where I got stuck, and how I got out

01

Simply adding up the prefecture and municipality figures counted resident tax deductions twice.

I split prefectures and municipalities into separate tables, and added tests that check every time that the national totals and all 47 prefectures match the Ministry's press release.

02

Drawing every remote island in its true position made the main islands too small to read, and excluding islands automatically by distance also dropped some mainland municipalities.

I named only the distant islands explicitly and listed them as name buttons outside the map.

03

The map data was from 2016, so municipalities that became cities afterward had new codes and were left uncolored.

I built a table that maps the changed codes, and confirmed that every municipality gets a color.
